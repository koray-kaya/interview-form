# Form 2.0 — the question set, in German and English

Date: 2026-09-23. Status: question set, order and rules approved by Koray in
the design session of the same day; German and English texts are drafts for
his review and then for a native-speaker check before the pilot. Implemented
in M3.5 (issue #15): this document replaces §3 of
`2026-09-21-interview-form-design.md`, and the form is `FORM_VERSION = "2.0.0"`.

## 1. Why the form changed

Form 1.0 had eight screens: profile, the four relations, one case with its
duration, pains, gains, contact. Reviewed against what the thesis needs from
its first stage, four things were missing and one framing was skewed.

- **The form now has to stand on its own.** The follow-up conversation is
  optional and many participants will decline it, so every figure the later
  impact evaluation compares against must come from the form: how often the
  work happens, how much working time it takes, what it costs in money, and
  whether the result was usable. Form 1.0 carried the depth (three open
  questions with follow-ups) and left the figures to the conversation.
- **Work that does not happen was invisible.** The thesis's value argument
  depends on telling two cases apart: firms that do this work at a cost they
  can describe, and firms that decide without looking because no affordable
  way exists. Form 1.0 only had "none of these".
- **Who does the work was not asked.** The outline leaves open whether a
  dedicated role exists in firms of 10–49 people, whether the owner does it,
  or nobody; the first stage is meant to settle that.
- **The case question leaned towards one job.** "Did you look into another
  company" (singular) makes people recall checking a supplier they already
  knew. The other job, searching for companies they do not know yet, is the
  one the product's first feature is about, and it could go unrecorded.
- **The unaided answer came after the categories.** The relations screen
  preceded the case, so the narrative arrived in our categories.

## 2. Principles

- **Unaided before aided.** The open case comes before the activity grid,
  and the open pains question before the aided list of things not done. The
  named source list is the last content screen.
- **Same categories twice.** The response categories shape what people
  report (Schwarz et al., 1985), so duration, cost, result and the frequency
  scale are re-used unchanged in the impact evaluation.
- **Rows, not a matrix.** On phones, item-by-item rows produce less missing
  data and less straight-lining than a grid table (Revilla, Toninelli &
  Ochoa, 2017). The frequency screen is five stacked rows, each with its own
  buttons.
- **Face-saving answer options** for the question about work not done: the
  reason sits inside the option ("because there was no time"), which recent
  evidence finds works better than a forgiving preamble.
- **Order:** easy closed items, then the three open questions in the middle,
  then the grid as a change of pace, then the aided lists, then contact
  (Thomas et al., 2024; Barari et al., 2024 on not probing early).
- **Three open questions, at most two AI follow-ups each.** Each follow-up
  prompt now names a primary and a secondary element, in that priority; the
  model asks for the highest-priority element that is missing and otherwise
  stays silent. Duration, cost, who and result are closed questions and are
  outside the model's scope.

Fourteen screens, eleven of them one tap, about ten minutes. The welcome
screen says so.

## 3. The screens

`Sie` throughout; Swiss spelling (ss). "Firma" rather than "Unternehmen": it
is the word people in this band use about themselves.

### Welcome and consent, one screen

The screen is short; the data-protection details sit behind a fold. The
folded text names the person responsible, the purpose, where answers are
stored and how to withdraw. The final wording goes through the ethics
approval; the audit's advice to name the processors on screen was
consciously not followed (decision 2026-09-23), and the answer to "where does
the AI run" goes into the ethics file rather than on the screen. On 2026-09-24
the sentence "answers are stored in Switzerland" was dropped: the daily export
goes to Vercel Blob and the follow-up model runs outside Switzerland, so it was
not true of every copy. The follow-up sentence now says "one or two", as
`maxFollowUps` allows.

**DE**

> **Wie Schweizer Firmen andere Firmen finden und prüfen**
>
> Ich bin Koray Kaya und schreibe meine Masterarbeit an der OST, der
> Ostschweizer Fachhochschule. Dafür stelle ich Firmen in der Ostschweiz eine
> einzige Frage: Wie informieren Sie sich heute über andere Firmen, etwa
> einen möglichen Kunden, einen Lieferanten oder einen Mitbewerber, und was
> kostet Sie das?
>
> 14 kurze Fragen, etwa 10 Minuten: drei zum Schreiben, der Rest zum
> Antippen. Es gibt keine richtigen Antworten; mich interessiert, wie Sie
> tatsächlich arbeiten.
>
> Ihre Antworten werden nur für diese Masterarbeit verwendet und ohne
> Firmennamen ausgewertet. Auf drei Ihrer schriftlichen Antworten können ein
> oder zwei kurze Rückfragen folgen; diese Rückfragen schreibt eine KI. Sie
> können jederzeit abbrechen.
>
> *Details ▸* Dieser Link ist für Ihre Firma bestimmt; ich nutze das nur, um
> Sie nicht zweimal einzuladen und um Sie zu erreichen, wenn Sie das
> wünschen. Verantwortlich: Koray Kaya, OST, koray.kaya@ost.ch. Wenn Sie den Referenzcode von der
> letzten Seite an diese Adresse schicken, werden Ihre Antworten gelöscht.

**EN**

> **How Swiss firms find and assess other companies**
>
> I'm Koray Kaya, and I'm writing my master's thesis at OST, the Eastern
> Switzerland University of Applied Sciences. For it, I'm asking firms in
> eastern Switzerland one question: how do you find out about other
> companies today, say a possible customer, a supplier or a competitor, and
> what does that cost you?
>
> 14 short questions, about 10 minutes: three to write, the rest to tap.
> There are no right answers; I want to know how you actually work.
>
> Your answers are used only for this thesis and reported without company
> names. Three of your written answers may get one or two short follow-up
> questions, written by an AI. You can stop at any time.
>
> *Details ▸* This link is meant for your company; I use that only to avoid
> inviting you twice and to reach you if you wish. Responsible: Koray Kaya, OST, koray.kaya@ost.ch. Send the
> reference code from the last page to this address and your answers will be
> deleted.
>
> ☐ I have read this and agree to take part. → **Start**

### Block A — profile, one tap each

**1 · `role` · single**
DE: Ihre Rolle · Inhaber/in oder Geschäftsführung · Verkauf · Einkauf · Andere
EN: Your role · Owner or managing director · Sales · Purchasing · Other

**2 · `size` · single**
DE: Wie viele Personen arbeiten in Ihrer Firma? · 1–9 · 10–49 · 50–99 · 100–249 · 250 oder mehr
EN: How many people work at your company? · 1–9 · 10–49 · 50–99 · 100–249 · 250 or more

**3 · `customers` · single** (new; the B2B selection criterion, which the
register cannot tell)
DE: Wer sind Ihre Kunden hauptsächlich? · Firmen · Privatpersonen · Beides, etwa gleich viel
EN: Who are your customers, mainly? · Businesses · Private individuals · Both, about equally

### Block B — the last case

**4 · `case` · open, probe ≤ 2, with an escape**
DE: Denken Sie an das letzte Mal, als Sie etwas über andere Firmen
herausfinden mussten. Was mussten Sie wissen, und wie sind Sie vorgegangen?
Help: Das kann eine bestimmte Firma gewesen sein oder die Suche nach Firmen,
die Sie noch nicht kannten; beides zählt. Beschreiben Sie einfach, was Sie
tatsächlich gemacht haben.
Escape: Ich erinnere mich an keinen solchen Fall.
EN: Think of the last time you needed to find out something about other
companies. What did you need to know, and how did you go about it?
Help: It may have been one particular company, or a search for companies you
didn't know yet; both count. Just describe what you actually did.
Escape: I can't think of such a case.
Probe: primary element, the steps taken and the sources used; secondary, what
exactly they needed to know. "I don't remember" is an answer.

**5 · `duration` · single** (now working time, not elapsed time)
DE: Wie viel Arbeitszeit hat das insgesamt etwa gekostet? · Unter 30 Minuten · Bis 2 Stunden · Etwa einen halben Tag · Etwa einen Tag · Mehrere Tage oder mehr
EN: Roughly how much working time did that take in total? · Under 30 minutes · Up to 2 hours · About half a day · About a day · Several days or more

**6 · `cost` · multi, "no" and "don't know" exclusive** (new)
DE: Hat das etwas gekostet, abgesehen von der Arbeitszeit? Help: Mehrfachauswahl möglich. · Nein · Ein bezahlter Auszug oder Bericht · Ein Abo oder eine Datenbank, die wir ohnehin haben · Externe Hilfe · Weiss ich nicht
EN: Did it cost anything, apart from working time? Help: Choose all that apply. · No · A paid extract or report · A subscription or database we have anyway · Outside help · I don't know

**7 · `result` · single** (new; the baseline for "usefulness of the result"
and for decisions taken without the information)
DE: Haben Sie am Ende gefunden, was Sie gesucht haben? · Ja, ich hatte, was ich brauchte · Teilweise, es blieben Lücken · Nein, aber ich habe trotzdem entschieden oder weitergemacht · Nein, und ich habe es aufgegeben
EN: In the end, did you find what you were looking for? · Yes, I had what I needed · Partly, there were gaps · No, but I decided or went ahead anyway · No, and I gave up

### Block C — reflection

**8 · `pains` · open, probe ≤ 2, context: `case`**
DE: Wenn Sie sich über andere Firmen informieren: Wo hakt es, wo dauert es
länger als nötig, wo geben Sie auf? Help: Ein konkretes Beispiel hilft am
meisten.
EN: When you look into other companies, where does it get stuck, take longer
than it should, or make you give up? Help: A concrete example helps most.
Probe: primary, a concrete situation; secondary, what it led to (waited,
decided blind, gave up). "Nothing gets stuck" is a finding.

**9 · `gains` · open, probe ≤ 2, context: `case`; skipped when the case was escaped**
DE: Rückblickend auf diesen Fall: Wie hätte ein wirklich gutes Ergebnis
ausgesehen, und was hätte es für Sie verändert?
EN: Looking back at that case: what would a really good result have looked
like, and what would it have changed for you?
Probe: primary, what decision or action the result would have changed;
secondary, what a good result would have contained. "Nothing" is a finding.

### Block D — the year

**10 · `activities` · five rows, one scale** (new; replaces `relations`)
DE stem: Wie oft kam das in den letzten 12 Monaten vor?
Rows:
- Neue Firmen gesucht, die Kunden werden könnten
- Neue Firmen gesucht, die Ihnen etwas liefern könnten
- Eine bestimmte Firma genauer geprüft: wer dahintersteht, was sie macht, ob sie verlässlich ist
- Mitbewerber im Auge behalten: wer was anbietet, und wo
- Die eigene Firma mit Mitbewerbern verglichen: Preise, Grösse, Angebot
Scale: Nie · 1–2 Mal · 3–6 Mal · Etwa monatlich · Wöchentlich oder öfter

EN stem: How often did this happen in the last 12 months?
Rows:
- Looked for new companies that could become customers
- Looked for new companies that could supply you
- Took a closer look at one particular company: who's behind it, what it does, whether it's reliable
- Kept an eye on competitors: who offers what, and where
- Compared your own company with competitors: prices, size, what you offer
Scale: Never · 1–2 times · 3–6 times · About monthly · Weekly or more

The first two rows are the discovery job (customer and supplier relation),
the third the deep dive, the fourth tracking (competitor relation), the fifth
own position. Analysis midpoints per year: 0 · 1.5 · 4.5 · 12 · 52.

**11 · `who` · single** (new; the structural question behind outline §1)
DE: Wer macht das in Ihrer Firma normalerweise? · Meistens ich selbst · Je nach Thema die zuständige Person: der Verkauf sucht Kunden, der Einkauf sucht Lieferanten · Dafür gibt es eine Person oder ein Team · Jemand von aussen, etwa ein Berater oder der Treuhänder · Niemand regelmässig; wer gerade Zeit hat
EN: Who usually does this at your company? · Mostly me · Whoever the topic belongs to: sales looks for customers, purchasing looks for suppliers · There's a person or team for it · Someone external, such as a consultant or our accountant · No one regularly; whoever has time

### Block E — aided lists

**12 · `skipped` · multi, "none" exclusive** (new; work that does not happen,
with the reason inside the option)
DE: Kam es in den letzten 12 Monaten vor, dass Sie … Help: Mehrfachauswahl möglich.
- bei einer Firma geblieben sind, die Sie schon kannten, weil eine Suche zu aufwendig gewesen wäre
- über eine Firma entschieden haben, ohne sie genauer anzuschauen, weil die Zeit fehlte
- eine Suche abgebrochen haben, weil Sie nichts Brauchbares fanden
- gern etwas über Firmen gewusst hätten, aber nicht wussten, wo nachschauen
- bewusst nicht gesucht haben, weil es nicht nötig war
- Nichts davon
EN: In the last 12 months, did it happen that you … Help: Choose all that apply.
- stayed with a company you already knew, because searching would have been too much effort
- decided about a company without looking into it, because there was no time
- gave up a search, because you found nothing useful
- wanted to know something about companies but didn't know where to look
- deliberately didn't search, because there was no need
- None of these

The fifth option is the control: work not done because it was not needed,
as opposed to work not done for lack of time, effort or a source.

**13 · `sources` · multi** (new; aided recall, after every open answer;
skipped when every row of 10 is "never")
DE: Wo haben Sie in den letzten 12 Monaten nachgeschaut oder nachgefragt, wenn Sie etwas über andere Firmen wissen wollten? Help: Mehrfachauswahl möglich.
- Website der Firma
- Direkt bei der Firma: Anruf, Besuch, Referenzen
- Handelsregister, z.B. Zefix
- Suchmaschine
- LinkedIn oder andere soziale Netzwerke
- Bezahlte Wirtschafts- oder Bonitätsauskunft
- Branchenverzeichnis, Verband oder Messe
- Persönliches Netzwerk: Kollegen, Kunden, Lieferanten
- KI-Assistent, z.B. ChatGPT
- Fachpresse, Medien
- Anderes
EN: In the last 12 months, where did you look or whom did you ask when you wanted to know something about other companies? Help: Choose all that apply.
- The company's website
- The company itself: a call, a visit, references
- Commercial register, e.g. Zefix
- Search engine
- LinkedIn or other social networks
- Paid business or credit report
- Industry directory, association or trade fair
- Personal network: colleagues, customers, suppliers
- AI assistant, e.g. ChatGPT
- Trade press, media
- Other

### Block F — contact

**14 · `followup` · multi + e-mail, "neither" exclusive** (unchanged)
DE: Wären Sie offen für … · ein 20-minütiges Gespräch · einen Test eines Werkzeugs in diesem Bereich später im Projekt · weder noch. E-mail label: Ihre E-Mail-Adresse.
EN: Would you be open to … · a 20-minute conversation · trying a tool in this area later in the project · neither. E-mail label: Your e-mail address.

**Thank-you screen:** unchanged (reference code, withdrawal address, "I will
be in touch" when a conversation was accepted).

## 4. Rules

- Skips: `case` escaped → skip `duration`, `cost`, `result`, `gains`.
  `activities` all "never" → skip `sources`. `pains`, `skipped`, `who` and
  `followup` are always asked.
- Exclusive options: `cost` "no" and "don't know"; `skipped` "none";
  `followup` "neither".
- Required: every screen; e-mail only when a `followup` option other than
  "neither" is chosen. Open answers capped at 4,000 characters.
- Probes: `case`, `pains`, `gains`; at most two model calls each, six per
  participant. `pains` and `gains` receive the `case` transcript as context.
- The repeatable core for the impact evaluation: `duration`, `cost`,
  `result`, and the `activities` scale, asked again unchanged for the tasks
  done with the system.
- If the pilot runs long, cut in this order: `customers` (the recruiting
  tool can check it from the website), `who` (role is a partial proxy),
  `sources` (the probe already collects sources unaided).
- Consciously outside the form, to be covered in the conversation guide and
  stated in the thesis: willingness to share the firm's own information (any
  form wording would describe the product), and the time from information to
  decision.

## 5. Translation notes

The German and English are written for the message, not word for word from
the Turkish working text.

- **"Firma", not "Unternehmen".** Owners of firms this size say "Firma";
  "Unternehmen" is the register's word and reads formal.
- **Title names both jobs**: "finden und prüfen" / "find and assess", so the
  discovery job is present from the first line.
- **Welcome speaks in the first person** and names the school, because the
  invitation comes from an OST address and the local name carries more trust
  than a data-protection paragraph. "Eastern Switzerland" rather than a town,
  so the text survives a change of municipality in recruiting.
- **The AI sentence is one clause**, "diese Rückfrage schreibt eine KI" /
  "written by an AI": disclosed, not dramatised.
- **Consent** reads as a person talking: "Ich habe das gelesen und mache mit."
- **`duration`** asks for "Arbeitszeit" / "working time" explicitly and
  drops "spread over weeks", which measured elapsed time, not effort.
- **`result`** became a yes / partly / no question in both languages; the two
  "no" options carry the decision-without-information and the abandoned
  search.
- **`activities` rows** are checklist participles ("Neue Firmen gesucht …"),
  which is how German forms list things a person did; the English rows are
  past-tense phrases for the same reason. Each row ends with two or three
  concrete words so that "checked a company" and "compared yourself" are not
  abstract.
- **`who`**: "Je nach Thema die zuständige Person" / "Whoever the topic
  belongs to" for the distributed case; "Treuhänder" is the Swiss word for
  the outside accountant most small firms have, "our accountant" in English.
- **`skipped`** keeps the "weil" / "because" clauses: the reason inside the
  option is the face-saving device, not decoration.
- **`sources`** gives one example only where the category would otherwise
  be unclear (Zefix, ChatGPT); no brand elsewhere.

## 6. What changes in code and prompts

- `src/form.ts`: `FORM_VERSION = "2.0.0"`; questions above; a new question
  type for stacked rows with one scale; an escape option on `case`; the two
  skip rules; the exclusive options on `cost`.
- `src/engine.ts` and the screens: the rows type and the escape control;
  the skip rules are data.
- `src/texts.ts`: welcome, details fold, consent, "14 questions, about 10
  minutes".
- `prompts/probe-case.md` version 2: the new question text; illustrative
  decisions include a discovery case (an exhibitor list, an association, a
  search for suppliers) beside the checking cases; secondary element added.
  `probe-pains.md` and `probe-gains.md` version 2: secondary element added,
  wording unchanged otherwise.
- `evals/fixtures.ts`: fixtures for discovery cases and for the secondary
  elements; the Turkish model answers from the design session become
  fictional German and English gold answers.
- The three version-2 prompt files are drafted in `docs/design/prompts-2.0/`
  (audited against Anthropic's Claude Sonnet 5 prompting guidance on
  2026-09-23: role line, form-level narration removed, two elements per
  question, examples in `<examples>` tags including discovery and
  language-mismatch cases, an explicit language rule that follows the
  person's answer, the fixed questions named as out of scope). They moved to
  `prompts/` together with `form.ts` 2.0, because the prompt test quotes the
  question text from the form.
- The prompts are at version 3 since 2026-09-24, after the first evals of
  version 2 (63 of 72 trials as expected). The model asked for the secondary
  element although the answer already held it ("after an hour I gave up",
  "we waited a week"), and twice offered answers to choose from. Version 3
  says that any trace of the secondary element counts, names waiting and
  giving up as what a difficulty led to, counts a purpose attached to a step
  as the information need, adds financial solidity to what a good result
  contains, and shows an open question beside an either-or one. Evals of
  version 3: 72 of 72, every follow-up in the language of the answer.
  Koray read version 3 in a Turkish rendering and approved it on the same
  day.
- Supabase: nothing; `form_version` and `question_text` already record what
  each participant saw.
